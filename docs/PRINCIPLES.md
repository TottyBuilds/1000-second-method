# Principles

What this is and what it isn't. Read this before complaining that it doesn't do something you wanted it to do.

## What this IS

**An installer, not a course.**
You run it once. It interviews you for about 12 minutes. It connects to the data sources you already use, then writes a personalized operating system into your workspace. From then on, the system lives in your files, plus reads from your own connected sources. Not from a server I run.

**A set of protocols, not a set of tools.**
A protocol is a verb. It is what you do, repeatedly, when you're tired. The six protocols here are the smallest set that durably shifts your week.

**A system that watches so you can focus.**
The installer connects to your calendar, your work messaging, your meeting transcripts, your physical tracking, and your family/personal calendar. Every morning it produces a Daily Punchlist: stack-ranked 1000-second actions across Physical, Mental, and Emotional pillars. Your job collapses to picking one and running it. The watching is the work the system does for you.

**Commitment infrastructure that records and reflects.**
The system runs logs. It does not nudge. It does not pester. It does not push you. It captures what you said you'd do, what you actually did, and what you stopped doing. It puts the signal in front of you. You make the calls.

**Yours, locally.**
Everything the installer generates lives in your workspace. The logs are your logs. The protocols are your protocols. The connected source data passes through your own Claude session and the third-party services you already authenticate with (Google, Slack, Granola, Strava, etc.). It does not pass through any server or database I control. The only thing that crosses a wire to me is whether you choose to opt into the Time Well Spent newsletter.

## What this is NOT

**Not a course.**
There are no videos. There is no curriculum. There is no LMS. You will not log in to anything to use it.

**Not a SaaS.**
There is no portal. There is no dashboard hosted by me. The files are yours. If you switch machines, copy the folder.

**Not a template you fill in.**
A template assumes someone else has already decided what the answers are. The wizard runs an interview because your week is not a template. Your placed sweep slot, your witness, your parked outcome, your pillar context -- none of these can be guessed. The rest the system learns by watching.

**Not paywalled.**
The installer is free. The protocols are free. The slash commands are free. The optional cloud routines are free. If you want to scale beyond solo execution, three tiers exist -- Every Expert, Backstage, Grit Collective. None of those gate the installer.

**Not a nudge machine.**
The system will not text you to "stay on track." It will not gamify your reps. It will not send streaks at you. The mechanism is not nudging. The mechanism is witnessing -- the witness is you reading your own logs, plus optionally a person you choose.

**Not built to replace your judgment.**
The 1000 Second Method does not tell you what to do with your life. It puts your time, your kills, and your commitments in front of you in plain text. The decisions remain yours. If you want a senior operator reading your logs alongside you, the graduation path exists.

## Operating principles for the system itself

**1. The six protocols are stable IP.**
The Daily 1000 (renamed from "The 1000 Second Sweep" in v2.2.0), the Leverage Matrix, the Agent Brief, the Weekly Kill List, the Public Commitment Slot, and The 21 (added in v2.5.0) -- the CONCEPTS behind these protocols will not change. Names get sharpened when they're materially wrong, as happened with v2.2.0's rename of Protocol 1: "sweep" implied clearing through things, which is the opposite of putting 1000 seconds onto one thing. Renames will be rare and require a real reason. New protocols can be added in future versions.

**2. Three pillars, one floor.**
Physical, Mental, Emotional. Same framing as Grit Collective. Work lives inside Mental. The pillars are visual organization, not a daily gate. Floor stays one wedge a day, any pillar. Balance is surfaced over a 7-day window and never enforced day to day.

**3. Your data is sovereign.**
`/1000s-update` will never overwrite `OPERATOR.md`, your logs, your parking lot, or any slash command you added yourself. Updates surface diffs and ask you what to do on conflict. Connected source data lives in your Claude session and on the third-party services you already use. None of it passes through any server I control.

**4. Silent before active.**
The default opt-in for cloud routines is silent (background data prep, no notifications). Active routines (reminders, nudges) require explicit per-routine opt-in.

**5. Brent's voice carries through.**
The wizard, the protocol files, the punchlist, the graduation reflector -- all of it is written in the same voice. First person. Conversational. Specific. No hype. No em dashes.

**6. The newsletter and the installer co-evolve.**
Updates ship through Time Well Spent. Subscribers learn what changed and why. The newsletter has a reason to exist beyond essays because of the installer; the installer stays fresh because of the newsletter.

## Why these choices

I built this for the operators I actually work with. Director-to-VP product, engineering, and ops leaders, AI-fluent, already performing well, skeptical of hustle content. The choices above are downstream of one observation: this audience does not need more tools and does not respond to more nudges. They need clearer verbs and they need their own logs in front of them.

If you are not that operator, this might still work for you. But the choices were made with that person in mind.
